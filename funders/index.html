Explainer

How an STTR collaboration works

STTR is the Small Business Technology Transfer program. This is the general pattern from the SBA policy directive; each agency’s solicitation sets the rules for a given award. Numbers in brackets point to the sources below.

Try the work-share split

The agency’s solicitation states how the shares are measured, by total award dollars or labor hours[7], and this illustration is not an eligibility check.

The two shares together cannot add up to more than 100%.

Step 1 of 5

  1. The small business applies

    STTR awards go to a small business that carries out cooperative research with a research institution. The research institution is a partner, not the applicant.

    Purpose
    The program’s statutory purpose is “to stimulate a partnership of ideas and technologies between innovative SBCs and non-profit Research Institutions”, by awarding SBCs for cooperative R/R&D with Research Institutions. (SBC means small business concern.) [1]
    Who must qualify
    Each Phase I and Phase II awardee must qualify as a small business concern at the time of award. [2]
    Principal investigator
    The principal investigator’s primary employment must be with the small business or, for STTR only, the research institution, meaning more than one-half of their employment time. [3]

  2. The research institution partners

    The directive defines which organizations count as a research institution, and requires a written agreement between the small business and the institution before the award.

    Definition (nonprofit route)
    A research institution has a U.S. place of business and is a non-profit institution as defined in section 4(3) of the Stevenson-Wydler Technology Innovation Act of 1980, that is, “an organization that is owned and operated exclusively for scientific or educational purposes”, with no part of its net earnings benefiting a private shareholder or individual. A federally funded R/R&D center identified by NSF also qualifies. [4]
    Agreement before award
    Before receiving an STTR award, the small business must negotiate a written agreement with the research institution that allocates intellectual property rights and any rights to carry out follow-on research, and submit it to the agency with the proposal. [5]
    Who decides eligibility
    Eligibility is determined per solicitation. This page does not say that any particular organization qualifies. [4][6]

  3. The work is split by a minimum share

    Statute sets minimums for each side. The minimums leave part of the work unassigned, and the agency states in its solicitation how the shares are measured.

    Small business
    Not less than 40 percent of the R/R&D work. [7][8]
    Research institution
    Not less than 30 percent of the R/R&D work, performed by a partnering research institution. [7][8]
    How it is measured
    An agency can measure the effort using total award dollars or labor hours, and must explain in the solicitation how it will be measured. Deviations from the STTR requirements are not allowed, because they are specified in statute (15 USC 638(e)). [7]

  4. Three phases, two of them funded by STTR

    The programs follow a uniform competitive process of three phases.

    Phase I
    Feasibility-related experimental or theoretical R/R&D, to determine the scientific and technical merit and feasibility of the proposed effort. Period of performance normally should not exceed 1 year for STTR, though agencies may allow longer. [9][10]
    Phase II
    Continues the R/R&D effort from the completed Phase I. Its duration normally should not exceed 2 years, though agencies may allow longer. [11][12]
    Phase III
    Work that derives from, extends, or completes an effort made under prior SBIR/STTR funding agreements, but is funded by sources other than the SBIR/STTR programs. [13]

  5. Federal agencies run it

    Each participating agency runs its own program and publishes its own solicitations. Award amounts and deadlines are in those solicitations, not here.

    Agencies listing SBIR/STTR
    Department of Agriculture, Department of War (listed as DOW), Department of Energy, Health and Human Services (including NIH), NASA, and the National Science Foundation. [14]
    Agencies listing SBIR only
    Department of Commerce, Department of Education, Department of Homeland Security, Department of Transportation, and the Environmental Protection Agency. [14]
    Program authority
    The enrolled S. 3971 extends the SBIR and STTR programs to September 30, 2031. [15]

Step 4

What happens after you get in touch

These are the channels and expectations the contact page already states. They are not service-level promises.

Where your message goes
We route it to the appropriate research or administrative conversation.
What to include
The technical question, scope, timeline, data or facility constraints, and the decision the work must support. For federal questions, add the program, solicitation, technical area and desired outcome when available.
What to leave out
Never send classified, export-controlled, proprietary, unpublished sensitive, clearance-identifying, or protected personal information through a public form or email.
How long it takes
No response time has been confirmed, so none is promised here. If a call does not connect, use email.

Research glossary

Terms used across the research pages.

These terms are collected from the glossary on each program page: 107 terms from 29 program pages. The definitions are shown exactly as each program page words them, and nothing is added here.

Where more than one page defines the same term, each page's definition is listed separately under the term, with a link back to that page. Numbers in square brackets, such as [1], refer to the numbered sources at the foot of the page named under that definition. The research A–Z index lists the programs themselves.

A

Accelerometer

A sensor that measures acceleration, used in wearables to infer movement and activity.

From: Kinesiology

AI-enabled device

A medical device that includes software functions using artificial intelligence.

From: Medicine

Algorithm

A precise, step-by-step procedure for solving a problem.

From: Computer Science

Argo float

A robotic instrument that drifts with ocean currents and cycles between the surface and mid-depth.

From: Ocean Science

Atrial fibrillation

An irregular heart rhythm originating in the upper chambers of the heart.

From: Physiology

B

Balancing authority

An organisation responsible for matching electricity supply and demand in a region.

From: Energy

Benchmark

A reference dataset used to compare methods fairly.

From: Chemistry

A fixed set of tasks and answers used to compare systems.

From: Natural Language Processing

BIDS

Brain Imaging Data Structure, a standard way of naming and arranging neuroimaging files.

From: Neuroscience

Biobank

A large collection of biological samples and linked data from volunteers, kept for research.

From: Genetics

Biogeochemical

Relating to the chemical and biological processes of the ocean.

From: Ocean Science

Birth cohort study

A study that follows children born in a given period over many years.

From: Sociology

Brain-wide association study

A study linking differences in brain structure or function across people to differences in cognition or mental health.

From: Neuroscience

C

CARE Principles

Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics, a framework for Indigenous data governance.

From: Anthropology

CASP

Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction, a community experiment that tests prediction methods blind.

From: Biology

Catalogue

A table of earthquakes with times, locations and sizes.

From: Geology

Causal inference

Methods for estimating the effect of one factor on an outcome rather than just their association.

From: Economics

Cognitive architecture

A formal theory of the mind's overall structure, implemented as a runnable program.

From: Cognitive Sciences

Completeness and reliability

The fraction of real planets found, and the fraction of candidates that are real.

From: Outer Space

Computational model

A theory written precisely enough to be run, so that its predictions can be compared with data.

From: Cognitive Sciences

Computational social science

The use of computational methods on digital data to study human behaviour and social structure.

From: Sociology

Connectome

A map of the connections between neurons in a brain or brain region.

From: Neuroscience

Convex hull

In this context, the boundary of energetic stability against competing compounds.

From: Materials Science

Credibility revolution

The shift in empirical microeconomics toward research design as the main source of credible evidence.

From: Economics

D

Datasheet

A document describing how a dataset was made, what it contains and how it should be used.

From: Data Science

Density functional theory

A quantum-mechanical method for calculating the electronic structure of molecules and solids.

From: Chemistry

A quantum-mechanical method for calculating the properties of materials.

From: Materials Science

Differential privacy

A formal guarantee that an analysis reveals little about any single individual.

From: Data Science

Dynamics

The forces and torques that produce movement, such as those generated by muscles.

From: Kinesiology

E

Electrocardiogram (ECG)

A recording of the heart's electrical activity.

From: Physiology

Entscheidungsproblem

The 'decision problem' in logic, asking for a general method to decide whether a statement is provable.

From: Computer Science

Ethnography

Research based on sustained, close observation of how people live, speak and make meaning.

From: Anthropology

Exome

The protein-coding portion of the genome.

From: Genetics

External validation

Testing a prediction model on data from a different place or time than the data used to build it.

From: Medicine

F

FAIR data

Data that are findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable.

From: Data Science

FAIR Principles

Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable: guidelines for scientific data management.

From: Anthropology

False positive

A detection that the model reports but that is not an archaeological feature.

From: Archaeology

A signal that looks like a planet but has another cause.

From: Outer Space

A statistically significant result for an effect that does not exist.

From: Psychology

fMRI

Functional magnetic resonance imaging, which tracks changes linked to brain activity.

From: Neuroscience

Force field

A simplified model that estimates forces between atoms cheaply.

From: Chemistry

Formal competence

Knowledge of a language's rules and patterns.

From: Linguistics

Formal specification

A description of what a system must do, written in a precise mathematical notation.

From: Formal Methods

Functional competence

Understanding and using language in the world.

From: Linguistics

H

Hallucination

Text produced by a language model that is not consistent with the facts, here including legal facts.

From: Law

Generated text that is unfaithful to the source or to the facts.

From: Natural Language Processing

Heart rate variability

The variation in time between consecutive heartbeats.

From: Physiology

Heterogeneous treatment effect

An effect of an intervention that differs across groups or individuals.

From: Economics

High-risk AI system

A category defined in Article 6 of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, with further listing in its annexes.

From: Law

High-throughput

Automated and repeated at large scale.

From: Materials Science

I

Indigenous data sovereignty

The principle that Indigenous peoples hold rights and authority over data about their communities, lands and knowledge.

From: Anthropology

Inductive problem

A problem of drawing general conclusions from limited evidence, such as learning a word's meaning from a few examples.

From: Cognitive Sciences

Interpretable model

A model whose decision process a person can follow directly.

From: Machine Intelligence

K

Kinematics

The description of motion itself, such as joint angles and speeds, without reference to the forces involved.

From: Kinesiology

L

Language documentation

The collection and preservation of primary records of a language, such as recordings with transcriptions and translations.

From: Linguistics

Language model

A system that predicts likely text, trained on large amounts of writing.

From: Natural Language Processing

Lidar

Light detection and ranging: airborne laser scanning that measures ground surface and structures in three dimensions.

From: Archaeology

Light curve

A record of a star's brightness over time.

From: Outer Space

Load

The demand for electricity at a given time.

From: Energy

Logical qubit

A more reliable qubit encoded across many physical qubits using error correction.

From: Quantum Computing

Luminosity

In collider physics, a measure of how many collisions an accelerator delivers.

From: Physics

M

Machine learning

Methods by which computers improve at a task automatically through experience.

From: Machine Intelligence

Matched filter

A detection method that compares data with expected signal shapes.

From: Physics

Missense variant

A DNA change that swaps one amino acid in a protein for another.

From: Genetics

Mixed reality

A broad term for experiences that blend real and virtual content, with AR as one part of it.

From: Augmented Reality

Model card

A short document that reports what a model was built for and how it behaves.

From: Machine Intelligence

Model checking

Automatic exploration of every state a model of a system can reach, to see whether a property is ever broken.

From: Formal Methods

N

Neural network

A model built from layers of simple connected units whose strengths are adjusted during training.

From: Machine Intelligence

NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum)

Preskill's term for today's devices: tens to hundreds of qubits, with noise that limits what they can do [2].

From: Quantum Computing

Nuisance parameter

A quantity that must be estimated along the way but is not the one of interest.

From: Economics

O

Ocean heat content

The amount of heat stored in the ocean.

From: Ocean Science

Optical see-through display

A display through which the user sees the real world directly, with graphics added optically.

From: Augmented Reality

P

Partial differential equation

An equation describing how a quantity changes across space and time, common in physics.

From: Physics

Phase picking

Marking when each type of seismic wave, such as P or S, arrives at a sensor.

From: Geology

Photoplethysmography (PPG)

An optical method that senses changes in blood volume under the skin, used for heart rate in many wearables.

From: Physiology

Photovoltaic (PV)

A technology that converts sunlight to electricity.

From: Energy

Polygenic risk score

A number that combines the small effects of many genetic variants into one estimate of inherited tendency toward a trait.

From: Genetics

Polynomial time

A running time that grows as a fixed power of the input size, usually seen as efficient.

From: Computer Science

Pose estimation

Computer vision methods that locate a person's body parts in images or video.

From: Kinesiology

Post-quantum cryptography

Encryption that runs on ordinary computers but is designed to withstand quantum attack.

From: Quantum Computing

Potential-energy surface

The energy of a molecule or material as a function of where its atoms are.

From: Chemistry

Predetermined change control plan

A plan, reviewed with the original submission, describing changes a manufacturer intends to make to an AI device later.

From: Medicine

Preregistration

Recording the research question and analysis plan before outcomes are observed.

From: Psychology

Profile

A set of measurements taken at different depths at one place and time.

From: Ocean Science

Proof assistant

A program that checks a proof step by step and refuses to accept any step that does not follow from the rules.

From: Formal Methods

Protein structure

The three-dimensional arrangement of the atoms in a protein, which is closely tied to what the protein does.

From: Biology

Proteome

The full set of proteins an organism can make.

From: Biology

Q

Qubit

The basic unit of quantum information, the quantum counterpart of a bit.

From: Computer Science

The basic unit of quantum information. Unlike a classical bit, it can be in a combination of 0 and 1 until measured.

From: Quantum Computing

R

Random forest

A machine-learning method that combines many decision trees to make a prediction.

From: Agriculture

Reanalysis

A consistent reconstruction of past weather from observations and models.

From: Energy

Registration

Aligning virtual objects with the real world so they appear to sit in the correct place [1].

From: Augmented Reality

Remote sensing

Gathering information about land or crops from a distance, usually by satellite or aircraft.

From: Agriculture

Gathering information about the Earth's surface from aircraft, satellites or drones.

From: Archaeology

Replication

Repeating a study to see whether its result holds again.

From: Cognitive Sciences

Repeating a study's procedure with new data to see whether the original result appears again.

From: Psychology

Reproducibility

The ability of others to rerun an analysis from the same data and code and obtain the same result.

From: Archaeology

The ability of others to obtain the same results from the same data and code.

From: Data Science

Retrieval-augmented generation

A technique in which a model draws on retrieved documents when producing an answer.

From: Law

S

Seismogram

A recording of ground motion at one seismometer.

From: Geology

Single-cell transcriptomics

Measuring which genes are active in each individual cell rather than in a tissue sample as a whole.

From: Biology

SLAM (simultaneous localisation and mapping)

Building a map of an unknown space while working out where the device is inside it [4].

From: Augmented Reality

Smallholder

A farmer working a small plot, often with limited access to inputs and records.

From: Agriculture

SMT solver

A tool that decides whether a set of logical conditions can all be true at once.

From: Formal Methods

Surrogate model

A fast learned model standing in for a slow calculation.

From: Materials Science

T

Template matching

Detecting small earthquakes by comparing recordings to known events.

From: Geology

Total product life cycle

The whole span of a device's existence, from design through use and updates.

From: Medicine

Transcription bottleneck

The shortage of skilled time to transcribe recorded speech, which limits documentation of endangered languages.

From: Linguistics

Transformer

A neural network design that processes text using attention rather than step-by-step recurrence.

From: Natural Language Processing

Transit

A planet passing in front of its star, briefly dimming it.

From: Outer Space

V

Voluntary framework

Guidance organisations may adopt, without it being a legal requirement by itself.

From: Law

W

Wave function

The mathematical description of a quantum system's state.

From: Physics

WEIRD samples

Participants from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich and Democratic societies.

From: Psychology

Word embedding

A representation of words as points in a numerical space, so that relationships between meanings can be measured.

From: Sociology

Y

Yield

The amount of crop harvested per unit of land.

From: Agriculture

Z

Zero-shot

Asking a language model to perform a task without giving it task-specific training examples.

From: Sociology

Social Sciences

Anthropology Research

INSTAR anthropologists examine human cultural diversity, social organization, and evolutionary adaptation through ethnographic fieldwork, bioarchaeological analysis, and computational modeling. Our research bridges biological and cultural perspectives to illuminate how human societies form, change, and persist across environments and time periods.

Cultural Studies

Cultural Anthropology

Our cultural anthropologists conduct long-term ethnographic research in communities across six continents, documenting knowledge systems, ritual practices, and social structures. Current projects explore how indigenous communities integrate traditional ecological knowledge with scientific data to manage natural resources. We employ participatory research methods that ensure community members shape research questions and benefit from findings.

Evolutionary Research

Biological Anthropology

We study human biological variation, primate behavior, and hominin evolution through fieldwork and laboratory analysis. Our researchers use 3D morphometric analysis, stable isotope geochemistry, and ancient DNA extraction to reconstruct migration patterns, dietary transitions, and disease histories across the human lineage. Active field sites span East Africa, Southeast Asia, and the Caucasus.

Our anthropological research informs policy on cultural heritage preservation, indigenous rights, and public health interventions. We maintain active partnerships with museums, government agencies, and community organizations worldwide.
